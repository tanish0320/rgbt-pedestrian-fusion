01

When RGB goes dark, thermal keeps working

Mean RGB pixel intensity drops to 10.7 out of 255 — below the noise floor for any convolutional feature extractor. The same pedestrian remains a distinct, high‑contrast heat signature in the paired thermal frame, captured at the identical instant by a co‑registered sensor.

02

When heat signatures merge, RGB keeps them apart

The converse failure: two pedestrians standing close together thermally bloom into a single warm region, an instance‑merging error no thermal‑only detector can recover from. Sharp clothing‑color and edge contrast in the RGB frame separate them cleanly. Neither modality alone is sufficient — they fail in different places.

How the detectors were scored

Phase 2 benchmarks three detectors — RGB‑only, Thermal‑only, and the fused Baseline QFDet — all evaluated from the same provided pretrained QFDet checkpoint. Unimodal numbers come from feeding that real checkpoint a zeroed tensor for the missing modality, not a separately trained substitute model. Full methodology and write‑up: reports/phase2_unimodal_baseline.md.

Detection metrics, explained

Standard COCO object‑detection metrics. All are computed at IoU thresholds against the ground‑truth boxes verified in Phase 1.

mAP Mean Average Precision averaged over IoU thresholds 0.50–0.95 (step 0.05). The single headline detection number — higher is better, rewards both correct localization and correct confidence ranking.
mAP50 / mAP75 AP at a single fixed IoU threshold (50% or 75% overlap with ground truth). mAP50 is a looser, easier‑to‑hit bar; mAP75 demands tighter box localization.
mAPS / mAPM / mAPL mAP computed separately on Small (<32²px), Medium, and Large ground‑truth boxes — the same size buckets from the Statistics tab. mAPS is the number this whole project is ultimately trying to move.

Computational metrics, explained

Cost and speed — a detector that scores well but can't run in real time isn't a viable deployment.

FPS / Inference time Frames processed per second and milliseconds per frame, measured on the same GPU as everything else in this project (RTX 4060). Inverse of each other; both reported since either can be the more intuitive read depending on context.
Model size Checkpoint file size on disk (MB) — what you'd actually ship or load.
Params Total learnable parameters (millions) — a proxy for model capacity and memory footprint independent of input resolution.
FLOPs Floating‑point operations per forward pass (billions), at the model's 640×512 eval resolution — a resolution‑aware proxy for compute cost, independent of the specific GPU used.

What the numbers say

Four findings from comparing the three detectors, all traceable to the table above.

01

Fusion beats either modality alone, by a wide margin

Baseline QFDet (mAP 0.299) beats Thermal‑only (0.232) by 29% relative, and RGB‑only (0.042) by 7×. This is the clearest evidence in the whole project for why a fusion strategy is worth building — neither sensor alone gets close to the fused result.

02

RGB‑only collapses far more than Thermal‑only degrades

This isn't simply "thermal is the better sensor." QFDet's quality‑attention fusion gate learns a per‑pixel weighting for each stream assuming both are present. Zeroing one stream doesn't just remove its signal — it breaks the calibration the other stream's weighting depends on. The gate leans more heavily on thermal to begin with (this dataset skews toward low‑contrast, low‑light aerial scenes), so losing it hurts less than losing RGB.

03

Small‑object detection is the weakest metric everywhere

mAPS (0.144 fused, val) is barely half of mAPM and a quarter of mAPL — in every one of the three detectors, not just the weak ones. Matches Phase 1's dataset finding almost exactly: fusion alone doesn't solve the small‑pedestrian problem, motivating Phase 3's shallow‑level fusion work specifically.

04

Val consistently outperforms test, across all three detectors

Baseline QFDet: 0.338 (val) vs. 0.299 (test). Consistent with Phase 1's finding that the test split has a 2.57× higher share of small pedestrians than train — and small objects are the hardest case for every configuration above. A real distribution‑shift effect, not evaluation noise.
